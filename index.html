How the numbers are made

Admit chance = school's admit rate × position of his stats inside the admitted middle-50% band (0.35–2.0× multiplier), then adjusted by ECs, AP rigor, and hooks. Each adjustment is scaled by a holism factor (1 − admit rate): at Miami (19% admit) a strong EC profile boosts chance ~15%; at Arizona (87% admit, stat-driven) it does almost nothing, which reflects how those schools actually read applications. Test-blind schools (all UCs/CSUs) ignore SAT automatically. The same full model now drives the UC Reference tab — UCs use comprehensive (holistic) review, so ECs and rigor count there too. AP exam scores get only a small validation bump by design: colleges weight course rigor over exam results, and UCs don't use AP scores in admission at all (they matter later, for credit/placement). Bands: Safety ≥75% (would be surprised by a denial), Likely 55–75% (odds in his favor), Target 30–55% (genuine coin-flip), Reach <30%. Est. net/yr = Sticker COA (tuition + fees + housing + food + books/travel; UCs and CSUs use California in-state rates) minus estimated annual merit for his band; need-based aid and ~3–4%/yr tuition inflation are not modeled. Rating (1–10) is an objective quality index \u2014 80% the average of premed environment, research access, bio strength, and med placement, 20% the 4-yr grad rate \u2014 deliberately independent of your weight sliders. School admit rate vs program acceptance: every percentage in the bands is admission to the COLLEGE; direct-med programs (BS/MD, early assurance) accept a far smaller share, shown separately as an estimate on those cards, and the Attainable/Stretch/Restricted/Out program bands are Claude's qualitative judgment of that harder door.
Fit score (0–100) = weighted average of 10 sub-scores using your Weights-tab sliders: cost-vs-budget (net ≤75% of budget = 10/10, degrading past budget), premed environment, med-school placement, research accessibility, bio quality, geography (CA 10 / West 8 / rest 4.5), size match, merit generosity (merit ÷ sticker), honors college, social life. Admit chance is deliberately not part of fit — it's the separate color band, so a great-fit reach never hides behind a mediocre safety.
Research accessibility (1–10), defined: how easily a median premed — not a superstar — lands a real lab position or clinical role by sophomore year. Scored from: teaching hospital / med school physically on or adjacent to campus; undergrad-to-grad-student ratio (grad students absorb lab seats); formal undergrad research programs with guaranteed slots; and volume of clinical volunteering within transit reach. Utah, Pitt, SLU score high because the hospital is on campus; Cal Poly and Chapman score low because the medical ecosystem simply isn't there.
Social life (1–10), defined: campus energy (D1 sports, Greek/social scene), college-town or city quality, % of students living on/near campus, and weekend-emptiness risk. Entirely reputational data — treat ±2 as noise and verify with a visit.
True Cost formula: 4-yr net (COA inflated yearly, minus merit — or minus merit for only 2 years in the lose-renewal stress test) + med school scenario + simple-interest estimate on borrowed amounts (undergrad borrowings carried ~7 yrs, med ~4.5 yrs at your chosen rate) − earned income (campus job × 32 wks/yr × 4, paid summer stipends, +$40k lifetime co-op at Drexel/Northeastern) − gap-year savings (40% of salary). Financing order: income + outside scholarships + family cash flow (budget ×4) + 529 cover costs before anything is borrowed; interest accrues only on the borrowed remainder, which is why a $200k 529 cuts True Cost through the interest line even though the savings themselves aren't a "discount." AOTC adds $2,500/yr ×4 when enabled (income-capped — phases out above ~$180k joint). Scenario band shifts costs/income ±10–15%. All figures are planning estimates, not financial advice.
True Cost metrics & scope: Economic cost = everything the journey consumes, whoever pays. Out-of-pocket = economic cost minus the 529 already saved (new money). Borrowed = what remains after income + outside scholarships + family budget + 529. Scope: Starred shows your shortlist regardless of filters; Filtered follows the List tab. Clinical Access (1–10): hospital volunteering + shadowing reach for a median premed — hospital density in transit reach, county/teaching hospitals, and slot competition; reputational. Merit semantics: figures are published automatic tiers at his band only — competitive scholarships are excluded, so real outcomes can only beat the app, never trail it.
Data confidence: solid = published institutional data (admit rates, costs, published merit tiers). reputational = student reports, forums, softer sources (premed climate, social life, committee-letter practices). All figures are 2024–26 cycle estimates; the links on each card are the ground truth.

UC Reference — all 9 campuses, one application

The 9 UCs now also appear in The List (filter Type → UC) with full fit scores, costs, premed data, and table sorting — this tab stays as the quick strategy view.
Three UC facts that change strategy: UCs are permanently test-blind (his SAT does nothing here — the GPA slider drives these bands). UCs use their own GPA: 10th–11th grade A–G courses, weighted but capped at 8 honors semesters — often higher than unweighted. One application covers all 9 (4 PIQs from 8 prompts), filing window opens Oct 1, submit by Nov 30, 2026. Bands below are estimated from unweighted GPA and are conservative; majors matter (biology is more competitive than the campus average).

Application timeline — senior year 2026–27

The trap to avoid: many merit scholarship deadlines land Nov 1 – Dec 1, earlier than regular admission deadlines. Applying RD in January can silently forfeit $80–100k of merit over 4 years. Rolling-admission schools (Arizona, ASU, SLU, Pitt) reward applying in September–October.

Per-college deadlines

Four application platforms cover everything: the UC App (one app + 4 PIQ essays = all 9 UCs, due Nov 30) · Cal State Apply (one app, no essays = all CSUs, due Nov 30) · the Common App (one main essay reused everywhere + short per-college supplements where noted) · and own portals (Arizona, UT Dallas — quick, essay-light). BS/MD and early-assurance programs usually require an extra application on top, due early.
